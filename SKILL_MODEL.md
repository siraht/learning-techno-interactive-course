# Skill model

The course tracks two complementary things:

1. **Section completion** answers: “Which curriculum material have I moved through?”
2. **Skill progress** answers: “Which capabilities have I encountered, studied, practiced, and demonstrated?”

A lesson can work several skills, and a skill can be developed across many lessons. The resulting structure is a many-to-many graph rather than a second linear syllabus.

## Progress signal

| Evidence | Weight | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Encountered | 15% | A linked lesson was opened. |
| Studied | 45% | A linked lesson was marked complete. |
| Practiced | 25% | A linked exercise was checked or a practice-log entry was tagged with the skill. |
| Demonstrated | 15% | The learner selected a self-evidence level and recorded what supports it. |

The percentages are a navigation signal, not a psychometric claim. They make neglected capabilities visible without pretending that browser activity can certify musicianship.

## Evidence scale

0. **Not claimed** — no evidence recorded.
1. **Recognize it** — identify the concept when it appears.
2. **Explain it** — describe the mechanism in ordinary and precise language.
3. **Apply it** — use the skill intentionally in a new exercise or track.
4. **Demonstrate it** — repeat the result, diagnose failures, and provide an export or other evidence.

Each skill defines its own demonstration standard and prerequisites. Lesson pages link to every skill they work; skill cards link back to all contributing lessons.
